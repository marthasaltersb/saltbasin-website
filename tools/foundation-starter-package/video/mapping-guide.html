Salt Basin · Release tracker

Bring your own project system

Your team keeps working the way it works today: Jira, Azure DevOps, Linear, GitHub, a spreadsheet, Scrum, Kanban or stage-gate. You answer seven mapping questions once, then send status changes to the tracker in its format. The board then shows every feature, test round and bug the same way for every team.

What the tracker counts

Everything in your system lands on one of these five things, or is left out.

The seven mapping questions

Answer these in writing before connecting anything. Most mapping problems trace back to a missing answer here.

  1. What is a feature in your system?Pick one item type and the key that names it, for example a Jira epic key like PAY-120. Stories under it are not separate features unless each is tested on its own.
  2. Which of your statuses mean each stage?Map every status to a stage, to “not started” or to “done”. A status that maps to nothing is a gap; decide it now.
  3. What counts as one test round, and where do results come from?The tracker needs steps passed and steps total per round, from test cases, acceptance criteria or a checklist. “QA said OK” is not a result.
  4. How is a bug tied to its feature?A parent link, an issue link, a label or a field. Every bug resolves to exactly one feature.
  5. How do you tell a returning bug from a new one?A reopened bug keeps its ID. A new report of the same cause names the earlier bug. The tracker counts fix attempts per bug, so this matters.
  6. What needs a business decision rather than a fix?A “Needs PO decision”, “Needs info” or “Blocked: business” status. These go to the owner with the exact question and are never guessed.
  7. When does a stuck bug go to a person?By default, after 2 fix attempts that the re-test still fails. Your team can set its own limit.

Sending the updates

From CI or a script

Record each stage when it starts and when it finishes, with its result.

From a webhook or export

A small adapter reads status changes from your tool and translates them using your seven answers.

Example: Jira epic PAY-120 fails 2 of 12 checks on its first test pass, and triage files bug PAY-131.

node log.mjs start  runs/2026-10-pay validate:PAY-120:r1
node log.mjs result runs/2026-10-pay validate:PAY-120:r1 '{"passed":false,"stepsPassed":10,"stepsTotal":12}'
node log.mjs start  runs/2026-10-pay triage:PAY-120:r1
node log.mjs result runs/2026-10-pay triage:PAY-120:r1 '{"items":[{"id":"PAY-131","step":"Refund total","rootCause":"Tax not reversed","class":"defect"}]}'

The board then shows PAY-120 as failing at 10/12 in round 1, with one open bug.

Rules that don't bend

  • A stage that ends without a result shows as failed, never done.
  • A feature shows passed only when its latest round passed every step.
  • Bug IDs stay the same across rounds. Use your tool's ID.
  • A missing number shows as “not recorded”, never as zero.
  • Only labels, statuses, summaries and counts reach the board. Keep personal or customer data out of titles and summaries.
