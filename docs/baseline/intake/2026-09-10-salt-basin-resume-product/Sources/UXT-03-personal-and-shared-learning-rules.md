## Salt Basin — proposed personal and shared learning rules

These rules extend the captured requirements. Cross-user contribution defaults below are proposals for confirmation.

| | Personal learning | Shared Salt Basin learning |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Improve recommendations for that specific user. | Identify patterns that may improve recommendations across users. |
| **Information used** | Their preferences, experience, sources, approved wording, edits, applications, and outcomes. | Permitted contributions summarized across comparable applications and outcomes. |
| **What it learns** | What the user wants, what they attest to, which language they approve, and what has worked for them. | Which approaches are associated with outcomes for particular roles, industries, experience levels, and career transitions. |
| **Effect on recommendations** | Tailors searches and outputs to the user’s own history. | Suggests approaches for the user to consider, subject to their actual qualifications and preferences. |
| **Boundary** | Approval for one application does not automatically authorize using that wording everywhere. | Another user’s success never supplies evidence that this user has a skill or qualification. |

### Personal memory

Keep an application-specific snapshot of the actual resume language and other materials submitted. Later edits must not overwrite that historical record.

Connect each snapshot to its supporting sources, approvals, target job, recommendation settings, and known outcomes. Distinguish generated documents from documents actually submitted.

Let users distinguish an application-only edit from an update to their reusable profile or preferred language. Provide controls to inspect, correct, and remove saved preferences.

### Shared learning and contribution

**Proposed default:** personal learning is available without requiring participation in shared learning. Shared contribution is a separate, explicit opt-in.

Define separately whether contributions may support aggregate analytics, shared recommendation improvements, or model training. Do not treat permission for one purpose as permission for all three.

Do not expose another user’s identity, private documents, reference information, or identifiable resume passages. Removing names alone is insufficient protection; rare combinations of career details can also identify someone.

Shared findings must reflect sufficient comparable evidence before influencing recommendations. Minimum group sizes and evidence thresholds remain to be defined.

### Evidence and feedback quality

Track the source of each outcome and distinguish a confirmed rejection from an unknown outcome or no recorded response.

Account for relevant differences such as role, industry, seniority, and timing. Describe findings as associations unless there is evidence supporting a causal conclusion.

Repeated use of wording does not verify its truth. A successful application does not validate every claim within it.

Shared patterns may inform suggestions, but must never override user deal breakers, fabricate qualifications, or silently raise a user’s proficiency level.

### User-facing explanations

Make the basis of a recommendation understandable:

- “Based on your saved preferences.”
- “Based on language you previously approved.”
- “Suggested from patterns across participating users.”

Allow users to accept or reject suggestions and control whether their decision updates personal memory. Contribution controls should explain what stopping participation changes and what happens to previously contributed information.
